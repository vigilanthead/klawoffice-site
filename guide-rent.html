Rent Disputes in Korea

How foreign tenants can protect their deposit and resolve disputes

Presented by K LAW OFFICE

Understanding Korean Rental Structures

Korea has two primary rental arrangements that differ from systems in many other countries.

  • Jeonse (전세): A large lump-sum deposit is paid instead of, or with little, monthly rent. The lease requires the landlord to return the deposit at the end, but actual recovery can be at risk if the landlord or the property cannot support repayment.
  • Wolse (월세): A smaller deposit combined with monthly rent payments. Less deposit risk, but tenants still need to protect the deposit amount.

Protecting Your Deposit: Key Steps

  • Possession and address registration: A residential tenant generally obtains opposability against third parties after taking possession and completing resident registration, with effect from the following day. For a foreign tenant, alien registration and a report of change in place of stay can perform the resident-registration function when the statutory conditions are met.
  • Request a fixed date (확정일자): A tenant who has both the opposability requirements and a fixed date may obtain preferential repayment in an auction or public sale. A fixed date alone does not create priority, and actual ranking depends on the relevant dates and earlier security interests.
  • Check the property's registration certificate (등기부등본): Before signing or paying any deposit, check registered encumbrances such as mortgages, attachments, and provisional attachments that could threaten your deposit.

Important: Possession, the correct registration or place-of-stay report, a fixed date, and the order of earlier registered rights can all affect recovery. Complete the applicable steps promptly and check the registry before paying the deposit.

Common Disputes and Legal Remedies

  • Landlord refuses to return deposit: After the lease has ended and the deposit remains unpaid, you may consider a payment order, civil claim, or a leasehold registration order (임차권등기명령). The registration order is not a lien; once the registration is completed, it can preserve specified tenant protections after moving out.
  • Landlord demands early vacating: Without a contractual or statutory ground for termination, a landlord generally cannot unilaterally terminate a fixed-term lease before its expiration.
  • Property sold or foreclosed during tenancy: Recovery depends on when possession and registration became effective, the fixed date, earlier mortgages and other rights, the sale price, and statutory priority rules. Do not assume that registration alone guarantees full repayment.
  • Unauthorized deductions from deposit: A landlord claiming damage beyond normal wear and tear should substantiate both the damage and the amount deducted. Disputes over deduction amounts are common and can be contested.

Practical Steps if a Dispute Arises

  • Document all communication with the landlord in writing.
  • Photograph the property's condition at move-in and move-out.
  • If you rely on a leasehold registration order, do not give up possession or change the relevant registration before confirming that the court-ordered registration has actually been completed.
  • Korea's Housing Lease Protection Act (주택임대차보호법) provides specific protections — understanding which apply to your situation is critical.

How an Attorney Can Help

An attorney can review your lease agreement, advise on whether your deposit is adequately protected, and represent you in civil proceedings or mediation to recover unpaid deposits or contest unjustified deductions.
