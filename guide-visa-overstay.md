# Visa Overstay in Korea

Legal consequences and possible paths forward

Presented by K LAW OFFICE

## What Is a Visa Overstay?

A visa overstay occurs when a foreign national remains in Korea after the authorized period of stay expires. The visa used to enter Korea and the current status and period of stay are different concepts, so confirm the expiry date shown in the immigration record rather than relying only on the visa label.

## Possible Legal Consequences

-   **Notice disposition or criminal process:** A violation may be handled through a notice disposition requiring a penalty payment or may proceed to criminal enforcement, depending on the statutory procedure and the facts. A penalty payment under a notice disposition is not the same as a court-imposed criminal fine.
-   **Departure order, deportation, and immigration detention:** Immigration authorities may use different measures when their statutory requirements are met. Deportation and detention are not automatic consequences of every overstay.
-   **Future entry restrictions:** The Immigration Control Act permits denial of entry, including where five years have not elapsed since a person left Korea under a deportation order. The actual result is not determined by a simple overstay-duration table.

## Voluntary Departure and Temporary Programs

Do not assume that self-reporting or voluntary departure automatically reduces a penalty or removes an entry restriction. Special voluntary-departure programs are announced for limited periods with their own eligibility rules. The special program that ran from December 1, 2025 through February 28, 2026 has ended. Check current Korea Immigration Service notices or call 1345 before relying on any program.

**Current policy matters:** Administrative practice and temporary programs can change. Confirm the current position before visiting an immigration office, booking departure, or filing an application.

## Practical Steps

-   Confirm your recorded status and authorized period of stay, including any pending extension or change application.
-   Ask the Immigration Contact Center (1345) or the competent immigration office which current procedure applies to your circumstances.
-   Do not assume that an ordinary extension or change of status remains available after an overstay. Any route to regularize status is fact-specific and may be exceptional.
-   Keep copies of passports, residence cards, applications, receipts, notices, and records explaining why the overstay occurred.

## Ongoing Legal Issues While Overstaying

Foreign nationals who are overstaying may also face complications if they have pending civil or criminal matters in Korea. Leaving the country may affect your ability to pursue or defend those proceedings. If this applies to your situation, it is important to address the legal matters in a coordinated way rather than separately.

## How an Attorney Can Help

An attorney can review the recorded status, the cause and duration of the overstay, family and litigation ties, and any immigration notice already issued. The available response may involve departure planning, presenting relevant circumstances in an immigration procedure, or examining whether an exceptional route to remain or change status exists; no outcome is guaranteed.
